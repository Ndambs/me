The Many-Shaped Hour

It never comes the same way twice.

BN
Benson Ndambiri25 November 2026·4 min read

It never comes the same way twice.

Once it was gold grass and a slow, crowned patience. Once it was a mist that knew my name. Once it was a door left ajar in a kindly dark, a song with my own sorrow tucked inside it. The shape is a borrowed thing. Only the invitation stays the same: come closer, it is only beautiful.

But who taught the hunter that beauty opens doors? Perhaps we did, every time we mistook the glow for the goodness. Light, you will notice, has never once asked to be trusted. The oldest liar learned to dress in radiance, and he wears it still. Gold on a hook is still a hook.

So the lovely thing drew near, and I felt myself leaning. Then a hush rose in me that was not mine, quieter than the lure and truer. It did not argue. It only said: rise. The still voice outlives every roar.

And the rising was not strength. I had none to spare. Not by might, not by power, but by a Breath older than my lungs. Whose hands were those, then, that learned war in the dark? I had never once enrolled, yet my fingers were taught, as a child's are taught by a patient palm folded around them. Strength that is lent is never in short supply.

The shape fell, as shapes do. A skin is only a sentence the deceiver speaks, and beneath it lay a face, and the face had a name. A disguise is only as brave as the dark it hides in.

Why does the mask always slip after the surrender and never before? Perhaps because we do not see clearly by suspicion, only by being held. The wrestling was never with blood, and yet there was flesh on the ground, trembling. So what was the foe, if not the face? Was it the one who wore the mask, or the thing that wore them? And when you stood over what you had overcome, what rose in you: hatred, or pity, or prayer?

Test every whisper before you let it sit at your table. A heart left unguarded is a city with its gates ajar. Wolves have always preferred wool, and the fleece was never stitched for the flock. Somewhere a roaring thing must keep walking, and that is the sign of its hunger, not its power. The hunted may rest. Lion and viper were promised a floor beneath your feet before they ever learned to rise from the grass. How many battles have you already won that you never knew were being fought?

Even the mask had its use. What was meant as a blade was handled by a better Hand and turned into bread. Even the dark was working for the dawn, and it did not know. Nothing is wasted in the hands of the One who wastes nothing.

Here is what the dream folds into its sleeve and keeps:

The war was already over.

Before the beast, before the mist, before the first mask was stitched, a Man hung between two thieves and the noon forgot its light. Heaven's whole argument arrived in three words, and the earth held its breath to hear them: it is finished. Every throne of shadow was stripped and led through the open street, disarmed, a captive in a procession it never meant to join. What comes now comes as silhouette, and the silhouette of a conquered king is still only a silhouette. A finished work asks no one to finish it.

So let the shapes come: the lion, the mist, the open door, the kindly voice. I will lift my eyes past the hills to the One who made them. I will wait, and in the waiting the weary will find their wings, rising like eagles above the hunting ground. The battle that frightens me is already being fought by Another, and my only task is to be still and let the sea part. In every storm, I am more than a conqueror, held by the One whose love outlasts every costume.

Walk on, then. Not fearless, but finished-for.

The beasts will keep arriving in new coats. The only question the field ever asks is the oldest one:

When they come, will you remember Whose you are?
